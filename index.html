A Beginner's Guide to Figure Skating: The World's Most Beautiful Sport

History, Notable Skaters, and Disciplines of Figure Skating

Figure skating is a sport in which individuals, duos, or groups perform on figure skates on ice. It was the first winter sport included in the Olympics, in 1908. The four Olympic disciplines are men's singles, ladies' singles, pair skating, and ice dance.

  • History

    • Originating in 18th-century Great Britain as a utilitarian way to navigate frozen canals, the sport transformed when American skater Jackson Haines introduced balletic movements in the 1860s. It eventually made history as the very first winter sport introduced to the Olympic program during the 1908 London Summer Games, long before the official Winter Olympic Games were created.

  • Notable Skaters

    • Legendary athletes have pushed the physical boundaries of the sport across different eras. American icon Michelle Kwan dominated the world stage with an unprecedented nine U.S. Championships and five World titles, German champion Katarina Witt secured back-to-back Olympic gold medals in the 1980s, and Japan's Yuzuru Hanyu became the first male skater in over half a century to win consecutive Olympic titles while mastering quad jumps.

  • Disciplines & Competitions

    • Modern Olympic events are categorized into four distinct disciplines that require highly specific skill sets. Men’s and Ladies’ singles focus heavily on explosive individual athletic jumps and complex spins, Pair skating introduces high-risk overhead lifts and synchronized throw jumps, and Ice Dance emphasizes complex footwork, musical rhythm, and ballroom technicalities without any overhead throws.

DAH SKATING FUNDAMENTALS

First Steps & Balance

  • Keep knees bent and weight centered over your skates
  • March in place to get used to the slippery ice surface
  • Practice basic forward gliding with your feet parallel

Gliding & Stopping

  • Push off using the inside edges of your blades to move
  • Gliding on a single foot helps build control and stability
  • Scrape the ice outward with your flat blade to snowplow stop

Getting Up From a Fall

  • Roll over onto your hands and knees immediately after falling
  • Place one foot flat on the ice between your hands and knees
  • Push down on your knee with both hands to stand completely up
